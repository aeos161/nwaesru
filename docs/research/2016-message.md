## 5. Gematria Primus sum analysis

### 5.1 Moving the final F and a Fibonacci/prime-index sequence

The proposed operation moves the `F` at the end of the message to the beginning. The final visible F occurs in the displayed key identifier `7A35090F`. The source invokes earlier uses of F as an interrupter to argue that moving it is less arbitrary. That precedent motivates an experiment; it does not establish that this particular movement is intended.

All rows of the article's first table are retained below. “Message association” records its annotations rather than independently recomputed GP sums. Prime indices use one-based indexing, with 2 as prime number 1.

| Fibonacci value | Prime index | Prime | Message association reported by source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 464 | 3301 | — |
| 1 | 463 | 3299 | — |
| 1 | 462 | 3271 | — |
| 2 | 460 | 3257 | — |
| 3 | 457 | 3229 | — |
| 5 | 452 | 3191 | — |
| 8 | 444 | 3119 | — |
| 13 | 431 | 3001 | 2016, sentences 1–3, +F |
| 21 | 410 | 2819 | 2016, sentence 4 |
| 34 | 376 | 2579 | 2016, remainder, −F |
| 55 | 321 | 2131 | 2017 message |
| 89 | 232 | 1459 | — |
| 144 | 88 | 457 | — |

**Editorial arithmetic check:** all displayed prime/index pairs are correct. The index column starts at 464 and then subtracts successive Fibonacci values cumulatively: `464 − 1 = 463`, `463 − 1 = 462`, `462 − 2 = 460`, and so on. It is not a separate subtraction of every listed Fibonacci value directly from 464. The word “Fibonacci” here describes the index steps; it does not mean the primes in the third column are themselves Fibonacci numbers.

The shifted sums are consistent with transferring value 2: `2999 + 2 = 3001`, `2581 − 2 = 2579`. This checks the arithmetic relationship between the tables, not the transcription, tokenization, or rationale for moving F.

### 5.2 Unshifted message sections

The source's second table is:

| Message | Sentences | Rune count | GP sum | Prime index |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2016 | 1–3 | 59 | 2999 | 430 |
| 2016 | 4 | 59 | 2819 | 410 |
| 2016 | Remainder | 59 | 2581 | — |
| 2017 | 1–2 | 45 | 2131 | 321 |

The source says the 2016 message can be divided into three groups of 59 runes and mentions including the A and F of the key identifier. It also describes sentence 4 and the remainder as 59 runes. Those reported counts depend on how Latin text, rune digraphs, numbers, headings, and the key identifier are handled. The article alone does not give a complete executable tokenization specification or reproduce the 2017 message text in this table.

**Editorial checks:** `2999` is prime number 430, `2819` is prime number 410, and `2131` is prime number 321. `2581 = 29 × 89` is composite, consistent with the dash in its prime-index column. Rune counts and GP sums have not been independently regenerated from either message.

The source uses italics and bold to distinguish sums and primes; the Markdown tables preserve their numerical values and associations explicitly rather than relying on typography.

### 5.4 Interpretations advanced by the article

The authors suggest that the analysis supports encryption operating on whole sections rather than individual pages, and that sections may behave as a ring or loop rather than a linear string. These are hypotheses drawn from patterns and the proposed final-F relocation, not demonstrated encryption rules.

They note that 59 is the 17th prime and that some solvers call the page referenced by the page-56 hash a “59th missing page.” They suggest a possible connection to the 59-rune groups but explicitly hedge its significance. “Missing page” is a solver label: the table does not establish that a new physical page exists or rule out the project's alternative hypothesis that the hash refers to content already in the corpus.

The article describes the analysis favorably as providing useful insight. That assessment is retained as the authors' opinion; the selected group boundaries, relocation, alphabet mapping, and comparison sequences need reproduction and consideration of alternative choices before claiming intentional design.
